2.1 The Big Task ⏱️ 0–10 min

Imagine you need to organize a school science fair with 30 projects and 200 visitors.

Is this one task or many tasks?

It is many tasks. Here is what needs to happen:

  • Find a venue.
  • Set up registration.
  • Arrange judging.
  • Organize projects.
  • Manage visitors.
  • Prepare food.
  • Plan awards.
  • Handle publicity.
  • Create a schedule.
  • Ensure safety.

Decomposition means breaking a big problem into smaller parts — and those parts into even smaller parts.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Have you ever tried to do a huge task all at once? What happened? What would have helped?

2.2 Multi-Level Decomposition ⏱️ 10–25 min

Here is the process:

  1. Step 1: Write the main problem.
  2. Step 2: Identify major sub-problems (level 1).
  3. Step 3: Break each sub-problem into smaller tasks (level 2).
  4. Step 4: Break those into even smaller tasks if needed (level 3).
  5. Step 5: Order them logically.
  6. Step 6: Check for overlaps or missing parts.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Use a tree diagram on the board. Write the main problem at the top and let students volunteer branches.

2.3 Example: Organizing a Science Fair ⏱️ 25–35 min

Main problem: Organize a science fair.

🌳 Level 1

Venue, Registration, Judging, Publicity, Food, Awards.

🌿 Level 2 for Judging

  • Recruit judges.
  • Create scoring rubric.
  • Assign judges to projects.
  • Collect scores.
  • Calculate winners.

🍃 Level 3 for Scoring rubric

  • Define criteria.
  • Assign weights.
  • Create score sheet.
  • Test with sample project.

The deeper you decompose, the easier each task becomes.

🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

Why do you think Level 3 is easier to do than Level 1? Discuss with your neighbor.

2.4 Decomposing a Class Trip ⏱️ 35–45 min

Problem: Plan a class trip to a museum for 45 students.

🌳 Level 1

Permission, Transport, Schedule, Money, Food, Supervision, Safety.

🌿 Level 2 for Transport

  • Choose bus company.
  • Get quotes.
  • Book bus.
  • Plan route.
  • Confirm pickup time.

🌿 Level 2 for Permission

  • Create permission form.
  • Send to parents.
  • Collect signatures.
  • Follow up with missing forms.

🍃 Level 3 for Permission form

  • Include date.
  • Include time.
  • Include destination.
  • Include cost.
  • Include emergency contact.
  • Include medical info.

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll build your own decomposition tree! 🦁

2.5 Group Activity: Decompose Your Own Problem ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work in a group of 4. Your teacher will give you a problem card:

  • Organize a school talent show.
  • Set up a school-wide recycling program.
  • Create a class yearbook with photos and stories.
  • Plan a healthy lunch menu for a week for 200 students.
  • Organize a book donation drive for a local orphanage.

Your task — create a decomposition tree with:

  • At least 5 level-1 sub-problems.
  • At least 3 level-2 sub-problems for each level-1.
  • At least 1 level-3 sub-problem.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Recorder: writes the tree structure.
  • Illustrator: draws branches, leaves, icons.
  • Presenter: explains the tree to the class.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to go deeper — "Can you break that down even more?" Ask them to justify why a task belongs at a certain level.

2.5b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will display their decomposition tree. Walk around and look at other groups' work.

As you visit each tree, ask yourself:

  • Did they identify the main problem clearly?
  • Are the level-1 branches distinct and logical?
  • Did they go deep enough (level 2 and 3)?
  • Is the order logical?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their tree to the class.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

2.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Decomposition means breaking a big problem into smaller parts.
  • Multi-level decomposition breaks sub-problems into even smaller tasks.
  • A decomposition tree shows the structure.
  • The deeper you decompose, the easier each task becomes.

2.8 Practice & Exit Ticket ⏱️ 97–100 min

Homework: Take one big task you have this week. Write 3 sub-problems. Then break one sub-problem into 3 sub-sub-problems.

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

In one sentence, explain what decomposition is.

Then draw a quick tree for this problem: "Plan a birthday party."

  • Level 1: ______________________
  • Level 2: ______________________
  • Level 3: ______________________
