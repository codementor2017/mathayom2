5.1 Whose Work Is It? ⏱️ 0–10 min

Look at a photo, a song, a drawing, and a poem.

Who owns these? Can anyone use them?

The creator owns them. Others can use them only with permission.

This is copyright.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Have you ever created something you were really proud of? Would you want others to use it without asking?

5.2 What is Copyright? ⏱️ 10–20 min

Copyright is the legal right of a creator to control how their work is used.

✅ Copyright protects:

  • Written works (books, articles, poems)
  • Visual works (photos, drawings, paintings)
  • Audio works (songs, recordings)
  • Video works (films, animations)
  • Software (code, apps)
  • Digital works (websites, designs, games)

❌ Copyright does NOT protect:

  • Ideas (only the expression of ideas)
  • Facts
  • Common knowledge
👩‍🏫 Tip: Clarify that copyright protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. Two people can write different stories about the same concept without violating copyright.

5.3 When Does Copyright Begin? ⏱️ 20–30 min

Copyright begins automatically as soon as you create the work in a fixed form. You do not need to register it.

In Thailand, copyright lasts for the life of the creator plus 50 years.

You own what you create. You have the right to decide who can use it and how.

5.5 Group Activity: Assert Your Ownership ⏱️ 60–85 min

You can create a copyright notice for your work.

A copyright notice includes:

  • Title of the work
  • Creator's name
  • Year of creation
  • Symbol: © 2025 [Your Name]. All rights reserved.
  • A short statement of what is allowed and what is not

Example:

"© 2025 Somchai Jaidee. All rights reserved. This work may not be reproduced, distributed, or modified without written permission from the creator."

Your task:

  1. Choose one of your own creations (a drawing, story, photo, code, etc.).
  2. Write a copyright notice for it.
  3. Include a short statement of what is allowed and what is not.
  4. Present your notice to the class.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Creator: describes the work.
  • Writer: drafts the notice.
  • Reviewer: checks clarity and completeness.
  • Presenter: reads the notice aloud.

Give one star and one wish.

5.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Copyright is the legal right of a creator to control how their work is used.
  • Copyright protects written, visual, audio, video, software, and digital works.
  • Copyright does not protect ideas, facts, or common knowledge.
  • Copyright begins automatically when you create the work.
  • You can assert ownership with a copyright notice.
