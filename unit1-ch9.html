9.1 Review the Four Pillars ⏱️ 0–10 min

What are the four pillars of computational thinking?

🧩

Decomposition

Break the problem into smaller parts.

🔍

Pattern Recognition

Look for patterns or similarities.

🎯

Abstraction

Focus on important details.

📋

Algorithm Design

Write step-by-step instructions.

Today we combine all four to solve one complex problem.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Which pillar do you think is the most important? Why? Share with your partner — there is no wrong answer!

9.2 Putting It All Together ⏱️ 10–25 min

Here is a complete example using all four pillars:

Problem: Organize a school library so students can find books quickly.

🧩 Decomposition

Fiction, non-fiction, reference, magazines.

🔍 Pattern Recognition

Most students look for books by genre and author.

🎯 Abstraction

Essential = genre, author, title. Non-essential = cover color, publisher.

📋 Algorithm

  1. Sort books by genre.
  2. Within each genre, sort by author's last name.
  3. Label each shelf.
  4. Create a catalog.
  5. Test with a student.
  6. Adjust based on feedback.

The four pillars work together.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Point out that each pillar builds on the previous one. Decomposition gives us the parts; pattern recognition helps us group them; abstraction helps us focus; and the algorithm puts it all into action.

9.3 Applying All Four Pillars ⏱️ 25–45 min

Problem: Plan a class exercise routine that everyone can follow.

🧩 Decomposition

Warm-up, cardio, strength, cool-down.

🔍 Pattern Recognition

Everyone can do basic movements.

🎯 Abstraction

Essential = time, movement type, repetitions. Non-essential = music choice, clothing.

📋 Algorithm

  1. 5-min warm-up.
  2. 10-min cardio.
  3. 10-min strength.
  4. 5-min cool-down.
🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

What other pattern might you notice in a good exercise routine? (e.g., start slow, end slow.)

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll build your own four-pillar solution! 🦁

9.4 Group Activity: Complete Computational Thinking Solution ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work in a group. Your teacher will give you a complex problem:

  • Design a system for the school cafeteria to reduce waiting time.
  • Organize a school event with 200 participants.
  • Create a study plan for 5 subjects in 2 weeks.
  • Design a recycling system for the school.
  • Plan a route for a school field trip.

You must:

  1. Decompose the problem into at least 3 levels.
  2. Identify at least 2 patterns.
  3. Create an abstraction (essential vs non-essential).
  4. Write an algorithm with at least 10 steps.
  5. Test with 2 cases.

Create a poster showing all four pillars. Use colors, icons, and clear labels.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Decomposer: leads the breakdown into sub-problems.
  • Pattern Finder: spots similarities and rules.
  • Abstractor: decides essential vs non-essential.
  • Algorithm Writer: writes the step-by-step plan.
  • Presenter: explains the poster to the class.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage groups to show how the four pillars connect on their poster. Use arrows or a flow diagram so the relationship between pillars is visible.

9.4b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will display their four-pillar poster. Walk around and look at other groups' work.

As you visit each poster, ask yourself:

  • Did they decompose the problem into 3 levels?
  • Did they find real patterns?
  • Did their abstraction make sense?
  • Was their algorithm clear and complete?
  • Did they test with 2 cases?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their poster.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

9.5 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • The four pillars work together.
  • Decomposition breaks the problem into parts.
  • Pattern recognition finds similarities and rules.
  • Abstraction focuses on what matters.
  • Algorithm design creates step-by-step solutions.
