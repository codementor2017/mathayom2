10.1 Project Overview ⏱️ 0–10 min

Now it is time to use everything you have learned. You will design a complete computational thinking solution for a real-life problem.

Your project should include:

  • A clear problem statement.
  • Decomposition with at least 3 levels.
  • Pattern recognition with at least 2 patterns.
  • Abstraction with essential and non-essential details.
  • An algorithm with at least 10 steps.
  • Testing with at least 3 cases (normal, boundary, edge).
  • A clear presentation.
🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Which pillar are you most confident in? Which one do you want to practice more? Share with your partner.

10.2 Project Options ⏱️ 10–20 min

Choose one:

  • Line up students by height quickly.
  • Arrange a wardrobe for easy finding.
  • Organize a school locker.
  • Plan a morning routine.
  • Design a library book system.
  • Organize a class folder system.
  • Plan a weekly meal schedule.
  • Design a school bag packing system.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Let students choose a problem that matters to them personally — they'll stay more engaged and produce better work.

10.3 Planning Your Project ⏱️ 20–45 min

Write down everything before you create your poster.

📝 Problem Statement

What is the problem you are solving? Why does it matter?

🧩 Decomposition (3 levels)

Level 1 — main sub-problems.

Level 2 — break each sub-problem into smaller tasks.

Level 3 — break at least one level-2 task further.

🔍 Patterns Identified

What similarities or repeating rules did you find?

🎯 Abstraction

Essential: what matters for the goal.

Non-essential: what can be ignored.

📋 Algorithm Outline

At least 10 clear, ordered steps.

🧪 Test Plan

Normal case, boundary case, edge case.

Check with your teacher before you continue.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Circulate and review each group's plan before they start creating. This prevents wasted time and ensures every pillar is covered.

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll create your project poster! 🦁

10.4 Creating Your Project ⏱️ 55–80 min

Create your project:

  • Poster, slides, or model.
  • Include all four pillars.
  • Include the algorithm with at least 10 steps.
  • Include a test with 3 different cases.

Prepare a 5-minute presentation.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Designer: draws the poster layout and pillars.
  • Writer: writes the algorithm and test plan.
  • Illustrator: adds icons, colors, and labels.
  • Presenter: prepares and delivers the 5-minute talk.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Remind students that the poster should clearly show the connection between the four pillars — not just four separate sections. Use arrows or a flow diagram.

10.5 Presenting Your Project ⏱️ 80–95 min

Your presentation should include:

  1. Problem and why it matters.
  2. Decomposition.
  3. Pattern recognition.
  4. Abstraction.
  5. Algorithm.
  6. Test results.
  7. What you learned.
🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

10.7 Reflection ⏱️ 97–100 min

After your presentation, write a reflection:

  • What was the hardest part?
  • Which pillar helped most?
  • How will you use computational thinking in the future?

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

Write one paragraph answering:

“How did computational thinking change the way I solve problems?”

🦁 “Remember who you are — a computational thinker!”

10.8 Chapter Summary

  • A computational thinking project uses all four pillars.
  • Testing with different cases makes the solution reliable.
  • Presenting helps you explain your thinking to others.
  • Reflection helps you learn from the experience.
