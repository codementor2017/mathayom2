1.1 Two Different Ideas ⏱️ 0–10 min

Read these two statements:

  • Safe use: "I don't get hacked."
  • Responsible use: "I don't harm others."

Are these the same? How are they different?

Safety is about protecting yourself. Responsibility is about protecting everyone.

You can be safe without being responsible. For example, you can use a strong password and never get hacked — but you might still post mean comments about a classmate. That is safe for you, but not responsible.

Responsibility goes beyond safety. It means thinking about the impact of your actions on others.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Can you think of an action that is safe but not responsible? Share with your partner.

1.2 The Four Responsibilities of a Digital Citizen ⏱️ 10–20 min

Here are four responsibilities from the Grade 8 indicator:

📢 Respond

When you see inappropriate content, act appropriately.

🛑 Prevent

Stop inappropriate content from reaching you or others.

🚫 Do not retaliate

Do not fight back with more harmful content.

📵 Do not spread

Do not share, like, or forward harmful content.

These four responsibilities protect the community.

1.3 An Example ⏱️ 20–30 min

A classmate posts an embarrassing photo of another student.

  • Safe response: Don't let it happen to you.
  • Responsible response: Don't like it. Don't share it. Report it. Support the target.

The difference is who you are protecting. Safety protects you. Responsibility protects the people around you.

1.5 Group Activity: Responsibility Case Studies ⏱️ 60–85 min

Work in a group. Your teacher will assign your group a scenario:

Scenario 1: A viral video mocks a student at your school.

Safe response: Don't watch it.

Responsible response: Don't share it. Report it. Support the student.

Irresponsible response: Share it, laugh at it, or make fun of the student.

Scenario 2: A fake account is impersonating a teacher.

Safe response: Don't interact with the account.

Responsible response: Report the account to the school and the platform.

Irresponsible response: Follow the account or share its posts.

Scenario 3: A classmate is spreading false rumors about another student.

Safe response: Don't get involved in the drama.

Responsible response: Tell a trusted adult. Support the target. Don't repeat the rumor.

Irresponsible response: Spread the rumor further.

Scenario 4: Someone posts a violent video in a group chat.

Safe response: Leave the group or mute it.

Responsible response: Report it. Tell an adult.

Irresponsible response: Watch it, share it, or comment on it.

Scenario 5: A friend shares a scam link that steals data.

Safe response: Don't click the link.

Responsible response: Warn your friend and others in the group.

Irresponsible response: Click it or share it with others.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Analyst: identifies the safe, responsible, and irresponsible responses.
  • Writer: records the group's answers.
  • Presenter: explains the scenario and responses to the class.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.

1.5b Sharing & Presentations ⏱️ 85–95 min

Each group presents their scenario and discusses:

  • What is the safe response?
  • What is the responsible response?
  • What is the irresponsible response?
🗣️ Peer Feedback:

Give one star and one wish.

1.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Safe use protects you. Responsible use protects everyone.
  • The four responsibilities are: respond, prevent, do not retaliate, and do not spread.
  • Responsibility goes beyond safety.
  • Every action has an impact on others.
