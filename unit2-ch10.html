10.1 Project Overview ⏱️ 0–10 min

Now it is time to use everything you have learned. You will design and write a complete program using logic and functions to solve a real problem.

Your project should include:

  • A clear problem statement.
  • An algorithm or pseudocode.
  • At least 3 functions.
  • At least 2 Boolean operators.
  • Correct logic.
  • A program that runs correctly.
  • Testing with 3+ cases (normal, boundary, invalid).
  • A clear presentation.
🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Which Unit 2 skill are you most proud of? Which one will you focus on in your project?

10.2 Project Options ⏱️ 10–20 min

Choose one:

  • Grade calculator with GPA.
  • Multi-variable inequality solver.
  • Triangle validity and classification.
  • Scholarship eligibility checker.
  • Loan approval system.
  • Movie ticket pricing with discounts.
  • Password strength checker.
  • Leap year and date validator.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to pick a problem that connects to their own life. Personal relevance = better engagement and better work.

10.3 Planning Your Project ⏱️ 20–50 min

Write down:

📝 Problem statement

What problem are you solving? Why does it matter?

📥 Input and Output

What goes in? What comes out?

📦 Functions to write

List at least 3 functions with their inputs and outputs.

🧠 Boolean conditions

List the conditions using and, or, and not.

📋 Pseudocode

Write your program in plain English first.

Check with your teacher before you continue.

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll test and present your program! 🦁

10.4 Coding and Testing ⏱️ 60–85 min

Write your program.

Test with 3 types of cases:

  • Normal case
  • Boundary case
  • Invalid case

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Writer: types the code.
  • Tester: runs normal, boundary, and invalid cases.
  • Recorder: fills in the debugging log.
  • Presenter: prepares and delivers the 5-minute talk.

10.5 Presenting Your Project ⏱️ 85–95 min

Your presentation should include:

  1. Problem and why it matters.
  2. Design (functions and logic).
  3. Program demonstration.
  4. Test results.
  5. What you learned.
🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star and one wish.

10.7 Reflection ⏱️ 97–100 min

After your presentation, write a reflection:

  • What was the hardest part?
  • How did functions help you organize your program?
  • How did Boolean logic help you make decisions?
  • What would you improve?

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

Write one paragraph answering:

“How did logic and functions help me solve a real problem?”

🦁 “You combined two superpowers — logic and functions. Keep coding!”

10.8 Chapter Summary

  • A complete program uses functions and logic together.
  • Testing with different cases makes the program reliable.
  • Presenting helps you explain your thinking to others.
  • Reflection helps you learn from the experience.
