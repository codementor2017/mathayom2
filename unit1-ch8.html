8.1 What Went Wrong? ⏱️ 0–10 min

Here is a flawed algorithm:

"To line up by height: everyone stands in a line and hopes for the best."

What is wrong?

  • There is no system.
  • There is no comparison.
  • There is no sorting.

Testing helps us find and fix problems in algorithms.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

What would happen if you followed this flawed algorithm? Share with your partner.

8.2 How to Test an Algorithm ⏱️ 10–25 min

Here are the steps for testing an algorithm:

  1. Step 1: Try a normal case.
  2. Step 2: Try a boundary case (e.g., all same height, one person, 100 people).
  3. Step 3: Try an edge case (e.g., missing data, duplicate heights).
  4. Step 4: Check the output.
  5. Step 5: Fix any errors.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Explain the difference between boundary and edge cases. Boundary = at the limit (e.g., 0 or 100). Edge = unusual situation (e.g., duplicate values, missing data).

8.4 Testing the Height Algorithm ⏱️ 40–45 min

Use the height-lining algorithm from Chapter 6.

Test with:

  • 5 students of different heights.
  • 10 students.
  • 2 students of the same height.
  • 1 student.
  • 100 students (simulate with cards).

Record results in a debugging log.

Did the algorithm work for all cases? What needs improvement?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll debug a flawed algorithm! 🦁

8.5 Group Activity: Improve an Algorithm ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work in a group. Your teacher will give you a flawed algorithm:

  • Algorithm 1: "To find a shirt, look in every drawer one by one."
  • Algorithm 2: "To sort books, put them anywhere on the shelf."
  • Algorithm 3: "To pack a bag, put things in randomly."

Your task:

  1. Identify the problem.
  2. Test with normal, boundary, and edge cases.
  3. Improve the algorithm.
  4. Write the improved version.
  5. Create a debugging log.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Analyst: identifies the problem in the flawed algorithm.
  • Tester: tries the algorithm with different inputs.
  • Fixer: writes the improved algorithm.
  • Recorder: fills in the debugging log.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to think of at least one boundary case and one edge case for their algorithm. For example, for Algorithm 1: "What if the drawer is empty? What if the shirt is in the last drawer?"

8.5b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will present their improved algorithm and debugging log.

As you listen, ask yourself:

  • Did they identify the real problem?
  • Did they test with all three case types?
  • Is their improved algorithm clear and complete?
  • Did their debugging log show good thinking?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their findings.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

8.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Testing helps us find and fix problems.
  • Test with normal, boundary, and edge cases.
  • A debugging log tracks your testing.
  • Good algorithms handle many different inputs.
