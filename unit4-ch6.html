6.1 Should Anyone Use Your Work? ⏱️ 0–10 min

If you made a drawing, would you let others use it?

What if someone wanted to sell it? What if someone wanted to change it?

These questions are about usage rights. Usage rights tell others what they can and cannot do with your work.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

If you created something amazing, would you share it freely, or would you protect it? Share with your partner.

6.2 Creative Commons Licenses ⏱️ 10–30 min

Creative Commons (CC) is a set of licenses that allow creators to share their work with specific permissions.

📜 CC BY

Others can use, share, and adapt your work if they give you credit.

📜 CC BY-SA

Same as BY, but they must share their version under the same license.

📜 CC BY-ND

Others can share but not change your work.

📜 CC BY-NC

Others can use it for non-commercial purposes only.

📜 CC BY-NC-SA

Non-commercial + share-alike.

📜 CC BY-NC-ND

Non-commercial + no changes.

📜 CC0

Public domain — no restrictions.

You choose the license based on what you want others to do with your work.

You can use the license chooser at creativecommons.org/choose.

6.3 Choose a License ⏱️ 30–50 min

Read each scenario. Which license fits best?

Scenario
Best License
A student photographer wants anyone to use their photos with credit
CC BY
A musician wants to allow remixes but only for non-commercial use
CC BY-NC
A writer wants their story shared but not changed
CC BY-ND
A teacher wants their lesson plan used freely by other teachers
CC BY or CC BY-SA
A game designer wants their game to remain free and open source
CC BY-SA or an open-source license
🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

Which license would you choose for your own work? Why?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll license your own work! 🦁

6.4 Group Activity: License Your Work ⏱️ 60–85 min

Choose a creation of your own.

Decide:

  1. What do you want to allow?
  2. What do you want to restrict?
  3. Which license fits?

Write a license statement:

"This work is licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0."

Include:

  • A link to the license.
  • Attribution instructions (how others should credit you).

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Creator: describes the work.
  • Decider: chooses the license and justifies it.
  • Writer: drafts the license statement.
  • Presenter: explains the choice to the class.

6.5 Why Choose an Open License? ⏱️ 85–90 min

Why would a creator choose an open license?

  • To share knowledge freely.
  • To help others learn.
  • To build a reputation.
  • To contribute to a community.
  • To allow others to improve the work.

Not every creator chooses an open license. Some prefer "All rights reserved." Both are valid choices. The important thing is that you decide.

6.5b Sharing & Presentations ⏱️ 90–95 min

Each group shares their license statement and reasoning.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

Give one star and one wish.

6.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Usage rights tell others what they can and cannot do with your work.
  • Creative Commons licenses let you share your work with specific permissions.
  • You choose the license based on your goals.
  • Open licenses help communities and build reputations.

6.8 Practice & Exit Ticket ⏱️ 97–100 min

Homework: Apply a Creative Commons license to a digital work you have created.
