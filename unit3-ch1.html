1.1 What Makes Something a Computer? ⏱️ 0–10 min

Think about the devices you use every day: a smartphone, a laptop, a smart TV, an ATM, a calculator, a smartwatch.

Are all of these computers? Why or why not?

A computer is a device that processes data. It takes input, works on it, produces output, and often stores the result. But a computer is not just hardware. It is a system — a group of parts working together toward a common goal.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Is a calculator a computer? What about a smart TV? Share your reasoning with a partner.

1.2 The Five Components of a Computer System ⏱️ 10–30 min

Every computer system has five components:

🖥️ Hardware

The physical parts. Examples: CPU, RAM, keyboard, monitor.

📀 Software

The programs and instructions. Examples: Windows, Word, games.

📊 Data

The raw facts and information. Examples: text, numbers, images.

🧑 People

The users who operate the system. Examples: students, teachers, employees.

📋 Procedures

The rules and steps for using the system. Examples: how to log in, how to save a file, how to report a problem.

A computer system is not just hardware. It includes software, data, people, and procedures working together.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Use a physical analogy — a computer is like a school. Hardware = the building; software = the lesson plans; data = student records; people = students and teachers; procedures = rules and schedules.

1.3 A Real-Life Example: A School Attendance System ⏱️ 30–50 min

Let's look at a school attendance system.

🖥️ Hardware

Computers, barcode scanners, ID cards.

📀 Software

Attendance application.

📊 Data

Student names, attendance records, dates, times.

🧑 People

Teachers, students, administrators.

📋 Procedures

Scan ID at the door, mark late arrivals, generate reports.

If any component is missing, the system fails.

  • Without hardware, there is nothing to run.
  • Without software, the hardware cannot do the job.
  • Without data, there is nothing to process.
  • Without people, no one uses it.
  • Without procedures, no one knows how to use it correctly.
🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

What happens if the data is wrong? What if the people are not trained?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll analyze a real system! 🦁

1.4 Group Activity: Analyze a Real System ⏱️ 60–85 min

Work in a group of 3–4. Your teacher will give you a system:

  • Online banking system.
  • School library system.
  • Food delivery app.
  • Traffic light control system.
  • Online game platform.

Identify all five components. Create a poster showing the five components.

Consider: What happens if the data is wrong? What if the people are not trained?

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Analyst: identifies each component.
  • Illustrator: draws the poster.
  • Presenter: explains the system to the class.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to think about failure points. What happens if a component is missing or broken?

1.4b Sharing & Presentations ⏱️ 85–95 min

Each group presents their system analysis poster.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

Give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

1.5 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • A computer system has five components: hardware, software, data, people, and procedures.
  • All five components work together.
  • If any component is missing, the system fails.
  • Analyzing a system means identifying all five components.
