1.1 The Messy Room Problem ⏱️ 0–10 min

Imagine your room is very messy. Clothes are on the floor. Books are piled on your desk. Papers are scattered everywhere. You need to clean it in 30 minutes.

What do you do?

Most people would naturally break the task into smaller parts:

  • Pick up the clothes.
  • Organize the books.
  • Sort the papers.
  • Make the bed.
  • Sweep the floor.

You just used computational thinking without knowing it.

Computational thinking is a formal way of solving problems like a computer scientist. It helps you break big problems into smaller, manageable parts and find efficient solutions.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Turn to a partner. Describe a time you broke a big task into smaller steps. What were the steps? Why did it help?

1.2 The Four Pillars ⏱️ 10–25 min

Computational thinking has four pillars:

🧩

Decomposition

Break the problem into smaller parts.

🔍

Pattern Recognition

Look for patterns or similarities.

🎯

Abstraction

Focus on important details; ignore the rest.

📋

Algorithm Design

Write step-by-step instructions.

These four pillars help us solve any problem efficiently.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Use a tangled ball of yarn (the problem) and small labeled blocks (sub-problems). Let students "untangle" it one piece at a time.

1.3 A Formal Example: Planning a School Sports Day ⏱️ 25–35 min

Let's apply the four pillars to a real problem.

Problem: Plan a school sports day.

🧩 Decomposition

  • Venue
  • Events
  • Participants
  • Equipment
  • Food
  • First aid
  • Schedule

🔍 Pattern Recognition

Last year's event worked well. We can reuse the schedule structure.

🎯 Abstraction

Essential: number of students, number of events, budget.

Non-essential: banner color, music choice.

📋 Algorithm

  1. Book venue.
  2. Open registration.
  3. Assign events.
  4. Prepare equipment.
  5. Run event.
  6. Evaluate.
🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

Why is "banner color" considered non-essential in the abstraction step? Share with your neighbor.

1.4 Applying the Pillars ⏱️ 35–45 min

Let's try another problem.

Problem: Design a system for a school cafeteria that reduces waiting time.

🧩 Decomposition

  • Menu design
  • Payment system
  • Seating
  • Serving stations
  • Queue management

🔍 Pattern Recognition

Peak times are the same every day. Popular dishes cause longer queues.

🎯 Abstraction

Essential: number of students, serving speed, payment method.

Non-essential: cafeteria wall color, uniform of staff.

📋 Algorithm

  1. Count students per lunch period.
  2. Measure serving time per student.
  3. Calculate number of serving stations needed.
  4. Assign stations.
  5. Test and adjust.
🗣️ Class Discussion (3 minutes):

What other patterns might you find in a cafeteria?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll work in groups to design your own computational thinking solution! 🦁

1.5 Group Activity: Computational Thinking Analysis ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work in a group of 3–4. Your teacher will give you a complex scenario. You have 25 minutes to complete the task.

Your task — apply the four pillars:

  • Decompose into at least 5 sub-problems.
  • Identify at least 2 patterns.
  • Create an abstraction (list essential vs non-essential details).
  • Write an algorithm with at least 8 steps.

Then create a poster showing all four pillars. Use colors, drawings, and clear labels.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Recorder: writes down ideas and steps.
  • Illustrator: draws the poster and icons.
  • Presenter: explains the poster to the class.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Circulate and ask: "What is the biggest part?" "Have you seen this before?" "What can we ignore?" "What's first?"

1.5b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will display their poster. Walk around and look at other groups' work.

As you visit each poster, ask yourself:

  • Did they decompose the problem clearly?
  • Did they find real patterns?
  • Did their abstraction make sense?
  • Was their algorithm easy to follow?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their poster to the class.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

1.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Computational thinking is a formal way of solving problems.
  • The four pillars are decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design.
  • These pillars help us solve problems efficiently.
  • Computational thinking applies to real-life problems.

1.7 Key Words

Word
Meaning
Computational thinking
A formal way of solving problems like a computer scientist
Decomposition
Breaking a problem into smaller parts
Pattern recognition
Finding similarities or repeating rules
Abstraction
Focusing on important details
Algorithm
A clear, ordered set of steps
Efficiency
Getting the best result with the least effort
