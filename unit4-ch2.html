2.1 What Would You Do? ⏱️ 0–10 min

Imagine you open a group chat and see a violent video. What would you do?

Some responses are helpful. Some make things worse.

  • Laughing makes it worse.
  • Sharing it makes it worse.
  • Reporting it helps.
  • Telling an adult helps.

How do you know what to do?

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Have you ever seen something inappropriate online? What did you do? Share with your partner.

2.2 The Four-Step Response Protocol ⏱️ 10–30 min

Here is a protocol you can always use:

Step 1: 📢 Report

Tell a trusted adult, use the platform's report button, or contact the relevant authority.

Step 2: 🛑 Prevent

Block the sender, adjust privacy settings, leave the group, or use content filters.

Step 3: 🚫 Do not retaliate

Do not respond with anger, insults, or more harmful content. Retaliation makes you part of the problem.

Step 4: 📵 Do not spread

Do not forward, share, screenshot, or repost the content. Sharing spreads the harm.

These four steps protect you, protect others, and stop the harm from growing.

2.3 What NOT to Do ⏱️ 30–40 min

  • ❌ Do not laugh or react.
  • ❌ Do not share with friends "just to show them."
  • ❌ Do not confront the person publicly.
  • ❌ Do not stay silent if someone is being hurt.

Silence can be as harmful as participation. If you see harm happening, you have a responsibility to act — even if that action is simply telling a trusted adult.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Reinforce that "not retaliating" doesn't mean "not acting." Reporting and blocking are actions — they just aren't harmful ones.

2.4 Applying the Protocol ⏱️ 40–50 min

Read each scenario. Apply the four steps.

Scenario 1: A threatening message from an unknown account.

Report to: A trusted adult or the platform.

Prevent: Block the account. Change your privacy settings.

Retaliation: Do not send threatening messages back.

Spreading: Do not share the message with others.

Scenario 2: A classmate posts a photo of another student without permission.

Report to: The platform or a teacher.

Prevent: Don't engage with the post.

Retaliation: Do not post something embarrassing about the person who posted it.

Spreading: Do not share the photo.

Scenario 3: A scam link in a class group chat.

Report to: The group admin or a teacher.

Prevent: Don't click the link. Warn others.

Retaliation: Do not spam the scammer back.

Spreading: Do not forward the link.

🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

Which step do you think is the hardest to follow? Why?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll role-play the response! 🦁

2.5 Group Activity: Role-Play the Response ⏱️ 60–85 min

Work with a partner. One person plays the person who received inappropriate content. The other plays a friend giving advice.

Demonstrate the four-step protocol in a 2-minute role-play.

Ask yourself:

  • Did they report?
  • Did they prevent?
  • Did they avoid retaliation?
  • Did they avoid spreading?

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Actor A: plays the person receiving the content.
  • Actor B: plays the friend giving advice.
  • Observer: checks that all four steps are covered.
  • Presenter: summarizes the role-play for the class.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Use realistic but age-appropriate scenarios. Keep role-plays short and focused on the four-step protocol.

2.5b Sharing & Performances ⏱️ 85–95 min

Each pair performs their 2-minute role-play.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each role-play, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

2.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • The four-step response protocol is: Report, Prevent, Do not retaliate, Do not spread.
  • Retaliation makes you part of the problem.
  • Spreading the content spreads the harm.
  • Silence is not an option when someone is being hurt.

2.7 Key Words

Word
Meaning
Inappropriate content
Content that is harmful, offensive, or violates rules
Report
Tell someone in authority about a problem
Prevent
Stop something from happening

Fight back with harm
Spread
Share with others
Protocol
A set of steps to follow
Escalate
Move the issue to a higher authority
