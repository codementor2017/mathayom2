7.1 The Morning Rush ⏱️ 0–10 min

Have you ever been late because you could not find the shirt you wanted?

Today we design a system to find any shirt quickly.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

How are your shirts organized at home right now? Is it easy or hard to find one quickly? Why?

7.2 Categorization and Sorting ⏱️ 10–25 min

Let's understand two important ideas:

🗂️

Categorize

Group by one attribute (type, color, season).

📊

Sort

Order within each group (by color, frequency, size).

📏

Rule

A clear instruction for where to put each item.

A good system has clear rules so you can find and return items easily.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Ask students: "What's the difference between categorize and sort?" (Categorize = grouping; Sort = ordering within groups.)

7.3 Designing a System ⏱️ 25–45 min

Here is an example system:

🗂️ Category 1: Type

T-shirts, long-sleeve, jackets.

🎨 Category 2: Color

White, black, colored.

⏱️ Category 3: Frequency

Daily, occasional, rare.

Now apply this to 12 sample shirts:

  • White T-shirt, black T-shirt, blue T-shirt, red T-shirt.
  • White long-sleeve, blue long-sleeve.
  • Black jacket, blue jacket.
  • Green polo, yellow polo.
  • Gray hoodie, red hoodie.
🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

Where would you put the blue long-sleeve shirt? How fast could you find it with this system?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll improve the system with data and labels! 🦁

7.4 Group Activity: Design a System ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work with a partner. Use the 12 sample shirts from Section 7.3.

Your task:

  1. Choose categories.
  2. Sort within categories.
  3. Write rules.
  4. Draw the wardrobe layout.

Test your system:

  • Where would you find the blue long-sleeve shirt?
  • How fast can you find it? (Time it!)
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to use a grid or diagram to show their layout. Label each shelf or drawer clearly.

7.5 Improving the System with Data ⏱️ 80–95 min

Work with a partner. Improve the system:

  • Add a label system.
  • Add a "most used" section at eye level.
  • Add a "seasonal storage" section.
  • Add a rule for new shirts.

Measure retrieval time for 5 shirts using your system.

Record data:

Test: Can someone else find a shirt in under 10 seconds using your rules?

🗣️ Class Discussion (5 minutes):

What made some systems faster than others? What role did labels play?

7.5b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will display their wardrobe system. Walk around and look at other groups' work.

As you visit each system, ask yourself:

  • Are the categories clear?
  • Are the rules easy to follow?
  • Would a new person understand the system quickly?
  • Did they measure retrieval time?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their system.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

7.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Categorization groups items by one attribute.
  • Sorting orders items within each group.
  • Rules make the system easy to use.
  • A good system is efficient and easy to follow.
  • Data helps us evaluate and improve the system.
