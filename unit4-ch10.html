10.1 Project Overview ⏱️ 0–10 min

Now it is time to use everything you have learned. You will create a digital work, apply a license, protect it, and present it.

Your project should include:

  • An original digital work.
  • A license chosen and justified.
  • A protection method applied.
  • A responsible sharing plan.
  • A response to inappropriate content protocol.
  • A clear presentation.
🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

What kind of digital work would you be most proud to create? Share with your partner.

10.2 Project Options ⏱️ 10–20 min

Choose one:

  • A digital illustration with a CC license.
  • A short story or poem with a copyright notice.
  • A photo series with a watermark.
  • A short instructional video with credits.
  • A piece of code with an open-source license.
  • A digital poster with attribution to used assets.
  • A music recording with a CC license.
  • A blog post with citations.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to choose a format they feel comfortable with. The focus is on the process (create → license → protect), not on the artistic skill.

10.3 Planning Your Project ⏱️ 20–50 min

Write down:

🎨 What work will you create?

Describe your digital work and its purpose.

📜 What license will you use and why?

Choose a Creative Commons license or "All rights reserved."

🔒 What protection method will you apply?

Watermark, metadata, timestamp, or another tool.

🌍 How will you share it responsibly?

Where will you publish it? What rules will you follow?

🚨 What will you do if someone uses it without permission?

Steps for response: contact, report, takedown, escalate.

Check with your teacher before you continue.

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll finish your project and prepare your presentation! 🦁

10.4 Creating Your Project ⏱️ 60–85 min

Create your digital work.

Apply your license and protection.

Write a short statement:

  • About the work.
  • About the license.
  • About responsible sharing.
  • About rights and responses.

Prepare a 5-minute presentation.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Creator: makes the digital work.
  • Licenser: chooses and applies the license.
  • Protector: applies the protection method.
  • Writer: writes the project statement.
  • Presenter: delivers the 5-minute presentation.

10.5 Presenting Your Project ⏱️ 85–95 min

Your presentation should include:

  1. The work and why you created it.
  2. The license you chose and why.
  3. The protection method used.
  4. Your responsible sharing plan.
  5. What you learned.
🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star and one wish.

10.7 Reflection ⏱️ 97–100 min

After your presentation, write a reflection:

  • What was the hardest part?
  • How did you balance creativity with responsibility?
  • What did you learn about ownership?
  • What would you improve?

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

Write one paragraph answering:

“How did this project change how I think about creating and sharing?”

🦁 “You are a creator — own it, license it, protect it.”

10.8 Chapter Summary

  • A complete project includes original work, license, protection, and responsible sharing.
  • Presenting helps you explain your choices.
  • Reflection helps you learn from the experience.
