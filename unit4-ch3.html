3.1 The Power of One Post ⏱️ 0–10 min

Imagine this: A student posts a joke about a classmate. Within 24 hours, 5,000 people have seen it.

How did this happen? What are the consequences?

The post was shared, liked, screenshotted, and reposted. It went viral. Even if the original poster deleted it, the screenshots remain.

This is the power of publishing. Once you post something, you lose control of it.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Have you ever seen a post go viral? What happened next? Share with your partner.

3.2 The Impact Framework ⏱️ 10–20 min

When you publish something, think about four types of impact:

🧑 Personal impact

How will this affect me now and in the future?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Impact on others

How will this affect the people in the post?

🌍 Social impact

How will this affect my school, family, or community?

⏳ Long-term impact

Will this still matter in 1 year? 5 years? 10 years?

3.3 Think Before You Post ⏱️ 20–30 min

Before you post, ask yourself:

  • ✅ Is it true?
  • ✅ Is it kind?
  • ✅ Is it necessary?
  • ✅ Is it safe?
  • ✅ Would I be okay if everyone saw this?
  • ✅ Would I be okay if this was about me?

If the answer to any question is "no," do not post it.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Encourage students to remember the four questions: True? Kind? Necessary? Safe? These are quick, memorable, and apply to any post.

3.4 Analyze Real Posts ⏱️ 30–50 min

Read each post. Analyze the impact.

Post 1: A photo of a friend sleeping with a funny caption.

Personal impact: Might seem funny now, but could embarrass you later.

Impact on others: The friend may feel humiliated.

Social impact: Others might think you are disrespectful.

Long-term impact: The photo could resurface years later.

Would you post this? Probably not.

Post 2: A rant about a teacher.

Personal impact: Could get you in trouble at school.

Impact on others: The teacher may feel hurt or angry.

Social impact: Creates a negative school culture.

Long-term impact: Future universities or employers could see it.

Would you post this? No.

Post 3: A screenshot of a private conversation.

Personal impact: You break trust and may lose friends.

Impact on others: The other person's privacy is violated.

Social impact: Creates drama and conflict.

Long-term impact: Could damage your reputation.

Would you post this? No.

Post 4: A video of a fight at school.

Personal impact: You could face school discipline.

Impact on others: The people in the fight are further humiliated.

Social impact: Encourages violence.

Long-term impact: Could affect your record.

Would you post this? No.

Post 5: A post celebrating a friend's achievement.

Personal impact: Builds positive relationships.

Impact on others: The friend feels supported.

Social impact: Creates a positive community.

Long-term impact: Shows you are supportive.

Would you post this? Yes! ✅

🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

What do the "No" posts have in common? What makes Post 5 different?

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 50–60 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll rewrite a problematic post! 🦁

3.5 Group Activity: Rewrite the Post ⏱️ 60–85 min

Work with a partner. Take a problematic post. Identify why it is problematic. Rewrite it in a responsible way. Explain what you changed and why.

Example:

Original post: "Look at how stupid my teacher is. Can't believe I have to sit in this class."

Problem: Disrespectful, could hurt the teacher, could get you in trouble.

Rewritten post: "Having a tough day in class today. Hope tomorrow is better."

Change: Removed the insult, removed the teacher's identity, focused on feelings instead of blame.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Analyst: identifies why the post is problematic.
  • Writer: rewrites the post responsibly.
  • Presenter: explains the changes to the class.
  • Timekeeper: keeps the group on schedule.

3.5b Sharing & Presentations ⏱️ 85–95 min

Each pair shares their original post, their rewrite, and their reasoning.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

Give one star and one wish.

3.6 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Publishing has personal, social, and long-term impact.
  • Once you post, you lose control.
  • Think Before You Post: Is it true? Is it kind? Is it necessary? Is it safe?
  • You can always choose not to post.

3.8 Practice & Exit Ticket ⏱️ 97–100 min

Homework: Review your own recent posts. Would you post them again? Write a short reflection.

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

In one sentence, explain why you should "think before you post."

Then list the four questions to ask before posting:

  1. Is it ______________________?
  2. Is it ______________________?
  3. Is it ______________________?
  4. Is it ______________________?
