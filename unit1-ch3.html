3.1 What Comes Next? ⏱️ 0–10 min

Look at this sequence:

2, 4, 8, 16, __, __

What comes next? How do you know?

The pattern is doubling. So the next numbers are 32 and 64.

Now look at this sequence:

1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, __, __

This is the Fibonacci sequence. Each number is the sum of the two before it. So the next numbers are 13 and 21.

Pattern recognition helps us predict and solve faster.

🗣️ Think–Pair–Share (2 minutes):

Can you think of another sequence you have seen before? (e.g., days of the week, multiplication tables, seasons.)

3.2 Patterns in Problems ⏱️ 10–25 min

Pattern recognition means finding similarities or repeating rules.

Here are some examples:

  • Math: Multiplication tables, geometric sequences.
  • Science: Day and night cycle, seasonal changes.
  • Daily life: Morning routine, traffic patterns.
  • Sorting: All shirts have sleeves; all socks come in pairs.

When we find a pattern, we can create a rule and reuse it.

👩‍🏫 Tip: Show a photo of a zebra and ask students to describe its pattern. Then ask: "What rule could describe this pattern?" (e.g., black-white-black-white stripes).

3.3 Patterns in Sorting ⏱️ 25–35 min

Problem: Arrange 10 random numbers from smallest to largest.

Here is a pattern we can use:

  1. Compare two numbers.
  2. Swap if the first is bigger.
  3. Repeat until sorted.

Let's see this in action:

5, 3, 8, 1 → 3, 5, 8, 1 → 3, 5, 1, 8 → 3, 1, 5, 8 → 1, 3, 5, 8

This is a pattern we can repeat.

🗣️ Quick Check (2 minutes):

How many swaps happened in the example above? Count them with your neighbor.

3.4 Patterns in Height-Lining ⏱️ 35–40 min

Problem: Line up students by height as fast as possible.

Pattern: Shorter people go to the front; taller people go to the back.

Rule: Compare each person's height with the next. Swap if they are in the wrong order. Repeat until sorted.

3.5 Patterns in Shirt Organization ⏱️ 40–45 min

Problem: Arrange shirts so you can find them easily.

Pattern: Shirts are worn more often if they are comfortable.

Rule: Put most-worn shirts at eye level.

🗣️ Class Discussion (2 minutes):

What other patterns could you use to organize a wardrobe? (e.g., by color, by season, by type.)

☕ Break Time ⏱️ 45–55 min

Stretch, hydrate, and reset your brain. When you return, you'll go on a pattern hunt with real data! 🦁

3.6 Group Activity: Pattern Hunt with Data ⏱️ 55–80 min

Work in a group of 3–4. Your teacher will give you a data set:

  • Class test scores across 5 subjects.
  • Daily temperatures for 2 weeks.
  • Heights of 20 students.
  • Number of cars passing the school gate each hour.
  • Minutes spent on homework each day for 2 weeks.

Find patterns in your data:

  1. What is similar?
  2. What is different?
  3. What rule can describe the pattern?
  4. What prediction can you make?

Write the pattern rule and your prediction. Be ready to explain your reasoning.

👥 Suggested Group Roles

  • Data Analyst: reads the numbers and finds similarities.
  • Rule Writer: writes the pattern rule in words.
  • Predictor: makes a prediction based on the rule.
  • Presenter: explains the findings to the class.
👩‍🏫 Tip: Ask students: "Is this a strong pattern or a weak one? How confident are you in your prediction?" This builds critical thinking.

3.6b Gallery Walk & Presentations ⏱️ 80–95 min

Each group will present their pattern rule and prediction. Listen carefully to each group.

As you listen, ask yourself:

  • Did they find a real pattern in the data?
  • Is their rule clear?
  • Does their prediction make sense?
  • Could their pattern be a coincidence?

Then each group's Presenter gives a 2-minute explanation of their findings.

🗣️ Peer Feedback:

After each presentation, give one star (something you liked) and one wish (something that could be improved).

3.7 Chapter Summary ⏱️ 95–97 min

  • Pattern recognition means finding similarities or repeating rules.
  • Patterns help us predict and solve faster.
  • When we find a pattern, we can create a rule and reuse it.
  • Patterns exist in math, science, daily life, and sorting.

3.9 Practice & Exit Ticket ⏱️ 97–100 min

Homework: Find one pattern at home (in your wardrobe, kitchen, or daily schedule) and write the rule.

🎟️ Exit Ticket — Complete before you leave

In one sentence, explain what pattern recognition is.

Then complete this sequence and write the rule:

3, 6, 12, 24, __, __

Rule: ______________________
